Design, Build, Ship · MPCS 51238 · Assignment 1

Shiftmap, twenty-five ways

One idea, designed 25 times: a country-of-origin research tool for sourcing teams. The page moves from wide exploration to a single final choice.

The product

Shiftmap answers one plain-language question by lining up a brand's sourcing records with industry import records. It returns a landscape report for a category.

Who it's for

Sourcing and category managers at apparel and consumer-goods brands who decide which countries and vendors to buy from.

A visitor should leave knowing

  1. where the sample category is made today,
  2. how that compares with the industry,
  3. which way to shift volume, and why.

Final choice

v25 will be pinned here once the process converges.

01

Go wide v01–v12

Radically different directions: layout, metaphor, era and tone. The same facts appear in every version, so only the design changes.

  1. v01shipping document · typewriter

    Bill of lading

    First direction. The page is the document sourcing managers already read every week: the origin mix is the cargo manifest, the recommendation is an amendment stamp, and "how it works" is the terms of carriage.

  2. v02map · pie charts · few words

    Flow map

    Answer to v01's feedback: too much text and not intuitive for business readers. The hero is now a map: bubble size is the share, a toggle switches between Northfold, the industry and the shift, and arrows draw the move. Three pie charts compare the mixes, and the reasons became three numbers.

  3. v03port yard · stencil · 1 box = 1%

    Container yard

    Every container is 1% of units, stacked by origin, so the mix is something you can count. Press Run the shift and a crane lifts 20 boxes off China and lands them in Vietnam and Cambodia. Stencil type, safety stripes, and container doors for the three reasons.

  4. v04split-flap · dark · signage

    Departure board

    Origins become departures on a split-flap board: share, a tile bar (1 tile = 4%) and an action column. Switching views re-flips the whole board, so China reads REROUTE −20 and Vietnam BOARD +12. Same facts, but now they arrive as a timetable.

  5. v05consulting · serif · one chart a screen

    Exhibit deck

    Written the way a sourcing director already reads: a cover that gives the answer first, then one exhibit per screen with a so-what title. A dumbbell shows the gap, ranked bars the industry, a waterfall the shift and vendor bars the capacity.

  6. v06garment labels · Bodoni · knit

    Care label

    The origin mix written the way every garment already states it: a woven label reads 52% China, 18% Vietnam, with industry ticks on each bar. The kraft hang tag flips over to show the after-shift pie, and the three reasons became care symbols.

  7. v07antique chart · engraved · sea routes

    Sea chart

    Back to a real map, but drawn as a 19th-century sea chart: waterlined coasts, graticule, compass rose, a cartouche for the title. Circles are 'parts in 100'; The new route draws dashed sea lanes from China to Vietnam and Cambodia with ships sailing them.

  8. v08brutalist · condensed black · one red

    Type is the chart

    No chart library, no map: each country's name is set at a font size proportional to its share. China shouts in red at 52%; after the shift it shrinks while Vietnam and Cambodia grow and invert to black. The bluntest version so far.

  9. v09data terminal · mono · dense

    Terminal

    First of four professional directions after v05 and v06 won the review. A trading-terminal screen with a ticker, a command line and five panels: mix bars, a diverging gap chart, key stats, the shift scenario and vendor leads. Keys 1–3 switch views, and the 25-pt over-weight shrinks to 5.

  10. v10broadsheet · serif · slope chart

    Front page

    The finding reported as news. A broadsheet front page leads with the headline and a slope chart (today to after the shift, or to the industry), with a rail for the trade's bars, a market-data table and a classified ad for vendors wanted. Professional, and the most information per screen so far.

  11. v11product UI · live mock · 4 categories

    Product window

    Shows the tool instead of describing it. The hero is a working app window with history, scope chips, KPIs, origin bars with industry ticks, a recommendation badge and vendors. Switch between four categories, or ask in English or Chinese and watch the report steps run.

  12. v12Swiss memo · print-ready · small multiples

    One-page brief

    The version you forward to a VP: a single printable sheet addressed TO/FROM/RE, numbered 01–06. The answer comes first, then small multiples for today vs the industry, stacked bars for the shift (with the industry as an optional third bar), three numbers, who to call, and the next step. Print or save as PDF.

02

Narrow down v13–v19

After v12, v05 (exhibit deck) was chosen as the spine, with v06 second, because both read as professional and carry enough information. The must-haves became a real map, clear data comparison and a professional tone. Each version below adds one strength borrowed from the exploration.

  1. v13v05 + v02 · cartographic · navy

    Deck + real map

    Narrowing starts here. The v05 deck stays as the spine, and its cover now carries a real map of South and Southeast Asia, drawn in the deck's professional palette. Bubbles show Northfold's share; Today, Industry and After shift switch the view, and the shift draws teal and amber routes that match the exhibit colours.

  2. v14v13 + v06 · composition ribbon

    Deck + map + label

    v06's best idea, made quieter: a woven-label ribbon under the cover (Made in · China 52% · Vietnam 18%…) with a small bar and an industry tick for each origin. It follows the map's Today / Industry / After toggle, so the cover now gives a picture and the exact numbers together.

  3. v15v14 + v10 · table + slope chart

    Deck + comparisons

    Stronger data comparison, which was one of the must-haves. Exhibit 1 pairs the dumbbell with a market-data table that adds the after-shift mix, freight per unit and net margin against peers. Exhibit 3 replaces the China-only waterfall with v10's slope chart, so every origin's move is visible; it can compare today with the shift or with the industry.

  4. v16v15 + v11 · try-it window

    Deck + live product

    Proof that the tool exists. A Try it slide before the recommendation puts v11's product window inside the deck, restyled to match: switch between four categories (each with its own badge: rebalance, renegotiate freight, add origin), or ask in English or Chinese and watch the steps run.

  5. v17v16 + v12 · memo header · 6-page PDF

    Printable deck

    Made to be forwarded. The cover gains v12's TO / FROM / RE memo line and a Print / save PDF button, and a print stylesheet turns the scrolling deck into a clean six-page A4 PDF, one exhibit per page with the interactive parts hidden.

  6. v18integration · sticky contents · keyboard

    Report navigation

    Everything from v17 stays, since all four additions earned their place, so this round is about reading the whole thing as one report. The side dots become a sticky contents bar (Answer, 1 Gap, 2 Industry, 3 Shift, 4 Vendors, Try it, Recommendation) with the current section highlighted and a progress line, and the arrow keys page through the slides like a deck.

  7. v19integration · one type scale · map labels

    Visual system

    A consistency pass on the same report. The repeated per-slide headers go, since the contents bar already says where you are (they come back as page headers in the PDF). Slide spacing is tighter, there is one focus style everywhere, and the cover map gains quiet cartographic labels (Myanmar, Thailand, Laos, India, the seas) so it reads as a real map, not a diagram.

03

Converge v20–v25

One direction, refined until it feels right.

  1. v20same content · v12 skin

    Bake-off A: Swiss

    Style bake-off, round one. v19's content and structure are unchanged, re-skinned in v12's Swiss memo style: one sans family, black and greys with a single red, square corners, hard rules. China is the only coloured thing on every chart, so the problem stands out immediately.

  2. v21same content · v11 skin

    Bake-off B: Product

    Style bake-off, round two. The same report in v11's product language: a dark navy hero, the map card turned into an app window, every exhibit a white rounded card on an app-grey canvas, pill segmented controls and system sans type. It reads most like software. Compare it with v19 (consulting) and v20 (Swiss).

  3. v22v20 · one figure, every key number

    Landscape board

    v20 won the bake-off. The first figure now carries the whole landscape: a KPI strip (sales, units, net margin vs peers, freight per unit, US imports), the map, and a detail panel that shows any origin's units, sales, industry imports, after-shift share, freight, margin and top vendor on hover, tap or keyboard. The composition ribbon sits underneath as the structure row.
